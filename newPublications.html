IoT and Machine Learning-based Energy-Efficient Smart Buildings

  • Automated Energy Control: Uses computer vision to detect human presence and automatically control the power supply to appliances.
  • Energy Efficiency: Reduces energy wastage by ensuring devices like lights and TVs are turned off when not in use or when users move away.
  • Cost-Effective: Significant savings on energy bills in the long run by minimizing unnecessary power consumption.
  • Flexible Usage: Suitable for various settings, including homes and offices, with adaptable configurations for different appliance types.
  • Enhanced Security: Sends alerts to users when someone enters a room after a specified time, providing an additional layer of security.
  • Privacy Protection: Monitors in real-time without storing any data, ensuring the user’s privacy is preserved.
  • Sleep Mode Detection: Automatically turns off appliances like TVs when users fall asleep, enhancing convenience.
  • User-Friendly: Designed for seamless integration with existing appliances and easy customization for specific needs.

    Fake news detection system using Machine Learning

  • Highlights the challenge of fake news classification in the age of media and mass communication.
  • Addresses the issue of falsified information reaching platforms with large audiences, such as news and radio channels.
  • Proposes a simple method to flag certain news pieces as likely fake for further investigation.
  • Focuses on news data surrounding the US 2020 elections.
  • Utilizes a Natural Language Processing (NLP) framework called BERT to extract contextual features from raw text data.
  • Classifies news as fake or real based on the headline and supporting text provided as input.
  • Demonstrates the effectiveness of the model through experimental results.
  • Published in Springer’s "Computational Methods and Data Engineering."

    Solar power forecasting to predict duck curve

  • Solar power is recognized as a key component of a green future, but excessive solar energy generation needs proper balancing to prevent damage to existing power sources.
  • Worked on the concept of the "duck curve," which represents the power required from non-solar sources during peak solar power generation.
  • Focuses on the calculation of the duck curve by predicting solar power generation using machine learning techniques.
  • Utilizes weather data parameters such as temperature, humidity, and wind temperature to predict solar output.
  • Enables efficient allocation of power demand to non-solar sources based on solar power generation trends.
  • Analyzes the typical pattern of solar power peaking during the daytime, followed by a sharp drop in non-solar demand and a steady increase as the sun sets.
  • Provides insights into adjusting non-solar sources for efficient power generation based on the duck curve.
  • Uses data from NSRDB to predict hourly Global Horizontal Irradiance (GHI) for VIT Chennai and corroborates findings with official sources.
  • Plots the duck curve for VIT Chennai and extends the model to observe GHI patterns in other locations across India.
